Where the water goes after it gets past the shingles

Water rarely drops straight to the floor. It runs along the underside of the roof deck, soaks the attic insulation, then follows a rafter or wire into the ceiling cavity and down inside a wall. The stain you see may sit far from the real entry point.

That path is why a small drip can wet a large area. Attic insulation holds water like a sponge and presses it against the drywall below until the board softens and sags.

Safety first when the ceiling is leaking

Do these before anything else

  • ✓If water is near a light fixture or fan, turn that circuit off at the panel, but only if you can stand dry to reach it.
  • ✓Call the utility or 911 for sparks or a burning smell.
  • ✓Stay out from under a sagging or bulging ceiling, because wet board can drop without warning.
  • ✓Do not climb onto a wet or icy roof.

A bulging ceiling: bucket first, release only if it is safe

A ceiling that bulges is holding water in a pocket above the drywall. Put a bucket under the lowest point and move furniture away. If the bulge is growing and the area is clear of fixtures and wiring, a small hole at the lowest point lets the water drain in a controlled stream instead of the whole section falling.

Keep well away from any light fixture or fan, because water follows wiring. If you are unsure, do not puncture it. Keep the bucket in place and call.

What you see and what it means

  • A brown ring that grows after each rain usually means a slow leak.
  • A ceiling that sags or feels soft means the board is saturated and will need to come out.
  • Peeling paint on an upper wall points to water inside the wall.
  • A musty smell in a room under the roof means moisture has stayed, so call even if nothing drips.

Roofer outside, restoration crew inside

Tarping and roof repair are roofer jobs, and you should call one early because the roof stays open until it is covered. We do not patch shingles. We handle the interior: removing wet insulation and ruined ceiling board, drying framing and the wall cavity, and cleaning the room.

Dry weather does not mean the damage has stopped.

How the inside gets dried

A crew starts by finding how far the water traveled, using a moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera to read the ceiling and walls. Saturated insulation and soft drywall are removed and bagged, and extraction units pull standing water from floors.

Then the drying equipment goes in. Air movers push air across framing and the open cavity, and LGR dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the room air. Readings repeat until the wood and board match a dry reference, following ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry reference.

Ice dams and missing shingles after wind

Ice dams are a different cause. Attic heat melts snow, the melt turns back to ice at the cold eaves, and the ice pushes water under the shingles. A roofer fixes the cause.

After a windstorm, look from the ground for lifted or missing shingles and bent flashing. Do not go up to check. Wind damage often shows inside first, as a stain after the next rain.

Insurance and your roof

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says standard homeowners policies usually cover sudden and accidental damage, and that wear, tear or neglect is not covered. A storm failure may be treated differently from a slow leak. Check your policy or ask your insurer, report the loss promptly and keep receipts for the tarp and repairs.
