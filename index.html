Water damage restoration for Hopkinton homes

Water damage restoration in Hopkinton, MA means getting water out, drying the structure until moisture readings are normal, and then repairing what could not be saved. Hopkinton Water Damage Pros runs a 24/7 line at (508) 446-9244 for burst pipes, failed appliances, roof leaks, basement flooding and storm water, and it connects you with a restoration crew for your address.

Water damage is time sensitive. Wet drywall, carpet padding and insulation can start to break down within a day or two, which is why the state Department of Public Health recommends drying wet areas within 24 hours. So the first call matters more than the first estimate.

First hour

What to do in the first hour

Whatever the source, the first hour is about safety and limiting the spread. These steps cost nothing and make the work that follows smaller.

While you wait for a crew

  • ✓Stop the source: close the main water valve, or the valve to the leaking fixture.
  • ✓Keep clear of any electrical hazard. If water is near outlets, the panel or a furnace, call the utility or 911 first.
  • ✓Lift valuables, papers and electronics off wet floors.
  • ✓Photograph the damage before moving anything, for your insurance record.
  • ✓Keep people and pets out of standing water, especially if it may be contaminated.

Local causes

Why water damage is common in Hopkinton

Hopkinton is a lakeside and wetland town. Whitehall State Park surrounds a 620-acre reservoir, Hopkinton State Park holds a 181-acre reservoir, and North (Maspenock) Pond covers 234 acres west of I-495. The Cedar Swamp Area of Critical Environmental Concern spans about 1,650 acres across Hopkinton and Westborough and stores floodwater for the Sudbury River basin, according to the state. In a town built around that much water, finished basements and low spots take on groundwater after long rain.

Winter adds burst and split lines. The National Weather Service's 1991 to 2020 normals for Worcester, the nearest regional station we found, show about 72.9 inches of snow a year, and melting snow on roofs is a regular cause of ceiling stains. The Census reports 86.7 percent owner occupancy, so most of the calls come from homeowners dealing with their own insurance questions.

Neighborhoods matter too. Hopkinton Center around the Town Common is a local historic district established in 1979, and older houses there tend to have plaster and original wood that need careful drying. Woodville, in the western part of town along Route 135, has its own historic district. Newer subdivisions have their own weak points, like roof valleys, bathroom fans and plastic supply lines.

Insurance

Insurance and water damage in Massachusetts

Standard policies and flood coverage are different

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance explains that homeowners policies generally cover sudden and accidental water damage but do not cover flood, and that sewer backup and sump pump failure usually need an added endorsement. Flood coverage is a separate policy that often takes 30 days to start. Check your policy, keep photos and invoices, and ask your insurer what applies.
