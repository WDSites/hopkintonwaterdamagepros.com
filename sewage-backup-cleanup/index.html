Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Hopkinton, MA

Category 3 water needs protective gear, removal of soaked materials and disinfection, with the line open around the clock.

☎ Call (508) 446-9244

A sewage backup in Hopkinton, MA is an emergency because the water is Category 3, which means it can carry bacteria and viruses. Sewage backup cleanup is the removal, disinfection and drying that follows. Right now, stay out of the affected room, stop using every toilet, sink, shower and washer, and keep children and pets away. Call (508) 446-9244, the 24/7 line. Same-day response is offered when crews are available.

Switch off power to the room only if the panel is in a dry spot, and call 911 for sparks or a gas smell.

Public line or private line: who you call first

If several fixtures back up at once, or neighbors report the same thing, the blockage may be in the public main under the street. The town's Water and Sewer Department handles public sewer mains during weekday office hours. After hours, emergencies go through Fire and Police dispatch, according to the town's DPW page.

If only one drain is backing up, such as a basement floor drain or the lowest toilet, the clog is more likely in the private pipe between your house and the main. That is a plumber's job. Many towns treat that lateral as the owner's responsibility, so ask the DPW before you pay for work. Clearing the pipe and cleaning up are separate trades.

What you see and smell, and what it means

Gurgling toilets and slow drains

Air is being pushed back by a blockage. Stop running water before anything overflows.

Dark water at a basement floor drain

The line is full and the water is leaving through the lowest opening. Treat it as sewage.

Sewer odor with a wet floor

A smell alone can be a dry drain trap. A smell plus wet flooring means a backup has happened.

Safety in the first hour

Do these before anything else

  • ✓Stop using water everywhere, including the washer and dishwasher.
  • ✓Stay out of standing sewage. If you must enter, wear boots, gloves and eye protection.
  • ✓Call 911 or the utility for sparks, buzzing or a gas smell, including a rotten-egg odor, since natural gas is given that smell.
  • ✓Keep children, pets and anyone with a weak immune system away.

Why Category 3 water changes every decision

Sewage cannot be dried and kept the way clean water can. The state environmental agency's home care guide says to assume pathogens are present, and porous materials soak them up and cannot be washed clean.

Septic homes, sewered homes and private wells

The town's Board of Health page lists Title 5 septic inspection forms, pumping record forms, a septage hauler list and private well permit forms, so septic systems and private wells are both in use here. We found no published figure for how much of town is sewered, so we will not guess.

The public facility the town lists is the Fruit Street Wastewater Treatment Facility, with a design capacity of 350,000 gallons a day and a current permit for 100,000. The page does not say which streets connect, so ask the DPW about yours.

In a septic home, a backup usually points to a full tank, a blocked outlet or a saturated leach field, so the first call is a septic pumper. Do not flush chlorinated wash water into the system, because it can harm the tank's bacteria. On a private well, if the cap was ever underwater, assume contamination, use bottled water or boil it for five minutes, and test at a certified lab.

The split shows up next door. Holliston's housing plan says the town has no town sewer system, so backups there are usually septic questions, as our Holliston area page notes. Ashland's plan reports public sewer serving about 91 percent of housing units, and the Ashland area page covers its town wells.

How the cleanup works

  1. Crews wear protective gear and seal off the room.
  2. Extraction units and pumps remove the liquid, and solids are bagged.
  3. Porous materials that touched sewage are cut out and removed.
  4. Surfaces are washed, disinfected and HEPA vacuumed, with negative air where odors could travel.
  5. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the space, and a moisture meter and thermal imaging camera confirm it.

The industry reference is ANSI/IICRC S500, which classes sewage as Category 3 water. For the general sequence, see our water damage cleanup page. When flood water mixes with a sewer line, see flood damage restoration.

What insurance usually says about sewage

The Division of Insurance says standard homeowners policies do not cover sewer or drain backup, though most insurers offer an endorsement, though some still exclude backups caused by flooding. Flood needs a separate policy, which usually takes 30 days to start. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and report a claim promptly.

Mistakes that make a backup worse

  • Running water to test whether the line has cleared.
  • Using a household vacuum or mop, which spreads contamination.
  • Mixing bleach with ammonia, which makes toxic fumes.
  • Adding a backwater check valve without first asking the sewer authority.
