Wall and Drywall Water Damage Repair in Hopkinton, MA

Wet walls assessed, dried or cut out, then rebuilt and finished to match, for homes in Hopkinton and nearby towns.

Drywall water damage repair in Hopkinton, MA is the work of deciding which wet wall board can be dried and kept, cutting out what cannot, and rebuilding the wall so it looks and performs as before. The line at (508) 446-9244 is open 24/7, and same-day response is offered when crews are available.

Water usually enters at the bottom of a wall, wicks up through the paper facing and soaks the insulation inside, so a wall that looks fine can still be wet.

Wet board or saturated board

Drywall is gypsum between paper faces, and both take on water. Board that is damp but firm, with no bowing, can often dry in place with air movers and a dehumidifier. Saturated board feels soft, crumbles at the edge, sags between studs or has a loose paper face.

Saturated board does not regain strength as it dries. Once the paper has lifted from the gypsum, or the board has stayed wet for days, cut it out. A moisture meter, not appearance, makes the call.

What you see and what it means

  • Paint that bubbles near the floor points to water wicking up the wall.
  • A tape seam that opens or ridges means the board swelled and moved.
  • A brown tide line means water passed through, and the cavity behind may still be damp.
  • A musty smell with no stain suggests damp insulation or framing behind the board.

Safety before you cut into a wall

Check these first

  • ✓Turn off power to the room at the panel from a dry spot, because outlets and wiring sit inside the wall.
  • ✓Call the utility or 911 for sparks or a burning smell.
  • ✓Wear a dust mask, gloves and eye protection.
  • ✓With old paint, ask first, because state lead rules apply when more than 6 square feet of painted interior surface is disturbed.

Drying follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the industry reference, with moisture meter readings repeated until they match a dry reference.

Flood cuts, insulation and baseboard

A flood cut is a straight horizontal cut through the wall above the wet line. Crews commonly cut at 2 feet, or at 4 feet when the water climbed higher or full sheets are easier to replace. The cut goes above the highest wet reading.

Baseboard comes off first and is saved when possible. Wet batt insulation is discarded. The stud bays are then dried with air movers aimed into the open cavity.

What to check behind the wall

Before new board goes up, the studs, bottom plate and exterior sheathing are read with a moisture meter. The Massachusetts Department of Public Health says wet porous materials should dry within 24 to 48 hours or mold growth is likely, and that you should not paint or caulk over mold.

If you see dark growth or smell mustiness, stop and read about mold remediation before you close the wall.

From open studs to a finished wall

Hang the board

New sheets go on once the cavity is dry and insulation is replaced.

Tape and mud

Tape and joint compound go on in thin coats, sanded smooth between coats.

Prime

Primer seals the new compound and the old paint so the finish coat dries evenly.

Match the texture

Texture is sprayed, rolled or hand-knocked to blend with the surrounding wall, then painted.

Matching is the hard part. Paint ages, so the finish coat often runs to a corner instead of ending mid-wall.

Plaster and the Hopkinton Center Historic District

Older houses often have plaster on wood lath, which dries slowly and is repaired differently from drywall. The town's own pages say the Hopkinton Center Historic District was established by townspeople in 1979, around the Town Common and the south side of Main Street. If your house is in or near it, ask the town whether work that touches the exterior walls needs review.

Plaster that is bowed from the lath or crumbling usually needs a plaster patch, not a drywall swap.

Permits and hiring rules in Massachusetts

Hopkinton's Inspectional Services page, at Town Hall, 18 Main Street, says a contractor supervisor license is not always required, but that this does not mean a permit is not required. Ask them before rebuilding.

The state's Home Improvement Contractor program requires registration for contractors working on owner-occupied one-to-four-unit homes. Work above the state's small-job threshold needs a written contract, and the deposit is limited to the greater of one-third of the price or the cost of special-order materials.

Insurance and walls

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance says standard homeowners policies usually cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe, but not flood, seepage through basement walls or wear and neglect. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and keep dated records and invoices together.

Mistakes that bring wet walls back

  • Painting over a stain before the wall has been read with a moisture meter.
  • Closing a wall over insulation that is still damp.
  • Skipping primer, which lets the patch show through.
