AC Leak Water Damage Repair in Hopkinton, MA

Drying and repair when an air handler, mini-split or window unit leaks into a ceiling, wall or floor.

AC leak water damage repair in Hopkinton, MA covers the wet ceilings, walls and floors that result when a cooling system drips or overflows. In New England this is a summer problem, and it is usually small until it runs for days in a hidden spot above a ceiling or inside a closet.

An honest note on how common this is here

In a snowy town, AC leaks are not the first cause people think of. Pipes, roofs and basements bring most water calls. AC leaks matter for a few months of the year, and mostly in homes with central air, heat pumps, ductless mini-splits or window units.

We are not claiming that central air is widespread in Hopkinton, because we do not have a sourced number. If your home has cooling equipment with a drain, this page applies.

Where AC water actually shows up

Clogged condensate drain line

Cooling pulls water out of the air, and a drain line slimed over with algae backs that water into the pan or the ceiling.

Overflowing drain pan

The pan under an air handler fills and spills onto the floor or through the ceiling below, especially in an attic or closet.

Frozen evaporator coil

Low airflow or a dirty filter can ice the coil. When it thaws, it drops more water than the pan can handle.

Mini-splits and window units

A mini-split indoor head with a pitched drain can leak down a wall, and a window unit tilted the wrong way drips inside.

What you see and what it means

  • A brown or yellow ring on a ceiling under an attic or closet air handler usually means the pan or drain line overflowed.
  • Water around the base of an indoor unit means the drain is backed up or the coil iced.
  • A musty smell in a closet or hallway near a cooling unit suggests damp material or growth.
  • A wet wall below a mini-split head points to a blocked or poorly pitched drain tube.

Safety when the unit is dripping

Steps to take right away

  • ✓Turn the thermostat off and switch off power to the air handler at the breaker if you can reach it dry.
  • ✓Do not touch the unit or its wiring if water is dripping onto it or the floor near it is wet.
  • ✓Call the utility or 911 for sparks, burning smells or a gas smell near a furnace.
  • ✓Call an HVAC technician for the system itself, since we dry the damage and do not repair cooling equipment.
  • ✓Put a bucket or towels under drips and write down when you first noticed them.

What to do about the drain line

Many air handlers have a capped access tee on the condensate drain line. A technician can flush it and clear the clog. If you can see the pan, check for standing water, and wipe up what you can reach.

Do not take the system apart yourself. Clearing a drain is simple. Finding why the coil iced or why the pan cracked is a trained technician's job, and running a system with a cracked pan will repeat the leak.

Why newer homes can still see wear

The town's 2004 housing plan found about 30 percent of its homes were built in the 1990s. The plan does not mention cooling equipment, so this part is an inference: newer homes have had time to see equipment wear, and a drain pan or line that is decades old can fail.

The National Weather Service's 1991 to 2020 normals for Worcester, a regional station, show 48.26 inches of precipitation a year. Humid summers load cooling equipment with water, but the equipment itself is the cause.

Repairing ceilings and walls below an air handler

Water from an attic or closet unit often soaks insulation and the ceiling drywall below it. Crews map the wet area with a moisture meter and thermal imaging camera, remove sagging drywall and wet insulation, and set air movers and LGR dehumidifiers.

The industry reference for this work is ANSI/IICRC S500. Condensate water is usually clean at first but can pick up dust and algae in the pan, so soaked insulation is replaced rather than dried.

Once dry, the room is rebuilt with drywall, primer and paint. Our page on water damage repair explains that rebuilding stage, and the ceiling is not repainted until readings are dry.

Mold risk and the 24 to 48 hour window

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health advises that wet areas should be completely dried within 24 hours, and says the Housing Code requires leak areas to be dried within 48 hours. AC leaks are often found late, so the window may already have passed.

If you see dark spots or smell mustiness, read about mold remediation before painting. The state guide also says to find where the moisture is coming from and fix it, and not to paint or caulk over mold.

Insurance and prevention

Standard policies usually cover sudden and accidental water damage and may treat gradual leaks differently. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and ask what documents they want.

Prevention is routine. Change filters on schedule, have the drain line flushed during the yearly service visit, and consider a float switch that shuts the system off when the pan fills.
