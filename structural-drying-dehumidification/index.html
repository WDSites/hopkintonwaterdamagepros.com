Structural Drying and Dehumidification in Hopkinton, MA

How drying is planned, measured and proven finished, from the first moisture map to the last reading.

☎ Call (508) 446-9244

Structural drying and dehumidification in Hopkinton, MA is the measured removal of moisture from framing, subfloors, plaster, concrete and block after water gets in. It is a science of evaporation and air handling rather than a matter of running fans. Call (508) 446-9244, which is answered 24/7, and same-day response is offered when crews are available.

Below we explain how drying works, how it is measured, what the daily logs should show and how you can tell for yourself when it is really finished.

Evaporation, humidity and temperature

Water leaves a wet material only by evaporating into air that can hold more of it. Three things control the speed: the surface temperature, how dry the surrounding air is and how much air moves across the surface. Raise warmth, lower humidity and keep air moving, and drying speeds up.

Air that is already humid cannot take up more water. Without removing moisture from the air, drying stalls and the water simply moves to a cooler wall or a closet.

Two kinds of dehumidifier

LGR and desiccant dehumidifiers compared
TypeHow it worksWhere it tends to fit
LGR (low grain refrigerant)Chills the air so moisture condenses on coils and drains awayWarm rooms, most house drying jobs
DesiccantPasses air over a moisture-absorbing material, then heats it to release the waterCold spaces, very low humidity targets, dense materials

Cold basements and winter jobs sometimes need a desiccant unit because refrigerant coils lose efficiency in cold air. Most homes are dried with LGR units. A crew picks the type from temperature, materials and readings.

Airflow patterns and containment

Air movers are not scattered at random. They are aimed to sweep air across wet surfaces and to push moist air toward the dehumidifier. On walls, air is directed along the surface.

The wet zone is contained with plastic sheeting and closed doors so humid air does not drift into dry rooms. That is also why windows stay closed. Opening them lets in outdoor air that may hold more moisture than the room, and it breaks the closed loop the dehumidifiers need.

Drying goals and the dry standard

A drying goal is a number, not a feeling. Crews take readings on materials that were never wet, such as an interior wall in a dry room, and use those as the dry standard for the house. Wet materials are dried until they match.

Different materials have different readings, so a drywall reading cannot be compared to a stud or a slab. Moisture mapping comes first so later readings can be compared like with like.

Moisture mapping

A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera mark the wet area, and each point is recorded.

Daily logs

Temperature, humidity and material readings are written down each visit so progress can be tracked.

Final readings

Equipment comes out only when wet materials match the dry standard.

Slow materials: hardwood, plaster, concrete and block

Thin drywall and carpet dry fairly fast. Hardwood, plaster, concrete and block do not. They hold water deep inside and release it slowly, so forcing them dry too fast can crack plaster or cup a hardwood floor.

Concrete slabs and block walls can read wet at depth long after the surface feels dry. Rushing to rebuild over them is how moisture problems return.

Hopkinton timing and climate

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health advises drying wet areas within 24 to 48 hours, and the state's Housing Code requires leak and flood areas to be dried within 48 hours. Slow materials may not finish in that window, but work has to start inside it.

The National Weather Service's 1991 to 2020 normals for Worcester, a regional station, show about 48.26 inches of precipitation a year and a mean temperature near 48.1 degrees Fahrenheit. Humid summers and cool seasons change how equipment is set up.

When drying has failed

Signs that drying has failed include a musty smell that returns, paint that bubbles again, a floor that keeps cupping and readings that stop falling. Ask for the daily logs and compare the readings with the dry standard.

If readings are stuck, check for a hidden source such as a slow leak, a closed wall cavity holding water or humid air leaking from outside. Sometimes insulation or a layer of flooring is trapping moisture and has to come out. If mustiness has set in, see mold remediation.

The industry reference for this work is ANSI/IICRC S500. You can ask any company how it sets drying goals, how often it logs readings and what it will show you at the end of the job.

Insurance and mistakes to avoid

Standard homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental water damage but not flood, and sump pump failure or sewer backup usually needs an endorsement. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and keep the drying logs with your claim records.

Mistakes include opening windows, switching equipment off because of noise and rebuilding before readings are normal.
