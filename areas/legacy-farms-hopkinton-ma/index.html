    Legacy Farms is newer housing, and its water problems look different from an old village. Most are plumbing, appliance and roof leaks, and many happen in buildings where several households share walls and floors. The line at (508) 446-9244 is open 24/7.

    This page is about Legacy Farms in Hopkinton. For the whole town, see water damage restoration in Hopkinton.

    What Legacy Farms is

    Hopkinton's 2017 Master Plan describes Legacy Farms as an overlay district of about 733 acres on former Weston Nurseries land. It allows up to 1,120 housing units and 250,000 square feet of commercial space, and requires at least 500 acres to stay open land. The plan notes that the commercial side was still an open question.

    Reporting by GBH in 2019 describes the housing as a mix of apartments, single-family homes and condominiums, and calls Legacy Farms North the town's densest housing yet. The Hopkinton Independent reported an active adult community there opening in October 2019.

    New construction, different leaks

    We have no source for foundation types or construction details in Legacy Farms, so we do not claim basements, slabs or specific materials. What is true of newer housing in general is that leaks come from supply lines, dishwashers, washing machines, water heaters, roof valleys and bathroom fixtures more often than from old pipes failing.

    That is why the first questions are practical. Where is the water coming from, is it still running, and does it reach a neighbor? Our pages on appliance leak water damage and water heater leak cleanup cover the most common causes in detail.

    Safety first: what to do in the first hour

    Do these in order

    • ✓Close the shutoff under the fixture, or the main valve if you cannot find it, and tell neighbors below and beside you.
    • ✓If a ceiling is bulging or dripping, keep people out from underneath it and do not poke it.
    • ✓Stay out of the room if water reaches outlets, and call the utility or 911 about sparks or a burning smell.
    • ✓Turn off a leaking water heater's power or gas only if the shutoff is dry and easy to reach.
    • ✓Call us for the water, and call a plumber or roofer for the source.

    Apartments, duplexes and shared walls

    A leak in a multi-unit building crosses boundaries. Water from one bathroom can drain into the ceiling below and travel along framing into the next unit. Our crews check the rooms around the leak with a moisture meter and thermal camera, because wet drywall does not always look wet.

    If you rent, tell the property manager in writing and keep a copy. If you own, ask your association whether it has a master policy and who reports the claim. We cannot decide who is responsible, and we do not file claims for anyone.

    How drying works

    After standing water is extracted, air movers push dry air across wet surfaces and low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air. In a modern sealed home, humidity can climb fast if the equipment is not sized right, so crews watch the readings daily. The industry reference is ANSI/IICRC S500, which we follow as a guide, not a certification.

    Flood maps and drainage: what we could not verify

    Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. We found no source that maps Legacy Farms to a flood zone, and we do not describe it as flood-prone. The Master Plan says at least 500 acres of the overlay district stay as open land, which is a planning fact, not a drainage finding.

    Heavy rain can find any building through a roof, a window or a grade problem, so we treat each job by its cause. Hopkinton's wetlands and soils are regional context, and the Master Plan says wetlands influence more than 30 percent of the town with buffers.

    What changes the cost

    Cost depends on the water category, how many square feet are wet, which materials are involved and how long drying takes. In a multi-unit building, the number of affected units adds scope. The crew lead explains the scope and price first.

    Insurance and mistakes to avoid

    The state Division of Insurance says standard homeowners policies generally cover sudden and accidental water damage but not flood, and that wear and neglect are not covered. Renters, condo owners and duplex owners each deal with different policies, so ask your insurer who covers what.

    Do not wait for a stain to dry before reporting it, and do not run fans over wet insulation and call it done. Keep dated photos and write down when you first saw the water.
