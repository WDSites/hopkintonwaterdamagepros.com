Wet basements, storm water and plumbing leaks in the old mill village along Wood Street.

    Where Woodville is and how it grew

    Hopkinton's 2017 Master Plan describes Woodville as a linear village along Wood Street, east of Whitehall Brook and north of the ponds that became Lake Whitehall. It grew around industries that used the hydropower of Whitehall Brook.

    A local television recording of a 2015 talk by the Friends of Whitehall says the Hopkinton Cotton Manufacturing Company was formed at the lower dam in 1811, and that the brick houses on Wood Street were built by Dexter and Elbridge Rice. We cite it as a local history source, not an official record.

    Older houses near a brook

    The Master Plan says Woodville has older multi-family buildings, and the town established a Woodville Historic District in 2005 covering 97 properties. A separate Woodville Historic District Commission exists. If you are inside the district, ask the town whether exterior work needs review before it begins.

    We do not claim older homes leak more. What older construction changes is the repair. Plaster, brick and old wood trim need a careful drying plan and a written list of what will come out. Ask for that list before work starts.

    Safety first: wet basement and storm water

    Do these in order

    • ✓Stay out of a flooded basement if water is above outlets, appliances or the furnace. Electricity and water together can kill.
    • ✓Call the utility or 911 for sparks, a burning smell or a gas odor before you do anything else.
    • ✓Treat water that came from a brook, a flooded yard or a drain as contaminated. Keep children and pets away from it.
    • ✓Do not use a household vacuum on standing water, and never run a gasoline pump or generator indoors.
    • ✓Photograph the water line from a safe spot, then call and describe what you see.

    Water, dams and what a hazard rating means

    Whitehall Reservoir is a 620-acre great pond inside Whitehall State Park at 300 Wood Street, according to the state. Hopkinton's 2017 hazard mitigation plan names Whitehall Brook among the town's waterways and lists several Whitehall dams, including the reservoir dam, which it rates as a significant hazard.

    A hazard rating describes what a failure could do downstream. It does not describe the dam's condition, and the plan says no dam breaches have been recorded in Hopkinton. We mention it for context, not as a flood warning for your street.

    What changes the cost

    Cost follows the water category, the wet area, the materials and the number of drying days. Dirty outside water adds removal and cleaning, brick and plaster dry slowly, and a finished basement costs more to dry than a bare one. The scope and price are explained before work starts.

    Insurance and prevention

    The state Division of Insurance says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, and that sump pump failure and sewer backup usually need an added endorsement. Check your own policy or ask your insurer before you assume anything.

    To reduce risk, clean gutters, point downspouts away from the foundation and test a sump pump before spring. None of that guarantees a dry basement, but it removes common causes.
