When water is spreading in Westborough

Westborough's hazard mitigation plan, written by the Central Massachusetts Regional Planning Commission and approved by FEMA in 2016, counted 215 properties exposed to a 100-year flood. You do not need to live on that list to have a bad night, because a failed washing machine hose does the same damage as a swollen brook. What matters is the first hour.

  • Find and stop the source

    If a pipe, tank or appliance line is leaking, close the main shutoff where the water line enters the house. Groundwater and storm water have no valve, so skip ahead to the next card.

  • Cut power only if the panel is dry

    If water is near outlets, cords or the electrical panel, do not wade in. Turn power off only if you can reach the panel without standing in water, and call the utility or 911 about any electrical or gas risk first.

  • Lift and photograph

    Raise furniture legs onto blocks, move rugs and papers upstairs, and photograph the water line and the damage before anything is moved. Those photos help the insurance claim.

  • Keep people and pets out of dirty water

    Water from a sewer line, a brook or a flooded yard should be treated as contaminated. Stay out of it and keep children and animals away until it has been removed.

  • Call the 24/7 line

    Call (508) 446-9244, say where the water is entering and how deep it is, and the dispatch starts while you keep working on the steps above.

Where Westborough's water problems start

The same plan lists streets and crossings where flooding has been recorded. They include the Warren Street, Morse Street and Upton Road area along Jackstraw Brook, Maynard Street near the Assabet River, East Main Street at the rail viaduct near Rutters Brook, and Route 135 at the Route 9 overpass.

If a basement fills after heavy rain on one of those streets, treat it as flood water rather than clean plumbing water. Surface and groundwater are handled as contaminated, which means more materials are removed and the scope grows. Our flood damage restoration page explains how that is judged.

Westborough also has dams, and the plan counts eight. The state-owned George H. Nichols dam carries a high hazard rating, and the town's two Sandra Pond dams are rated significant. A hazard rating describes what a failure would do downstream, not whether a dam is in poor shape, so it is not a reason to panic. It is a reason to look up your address on the FEMA flood map before you buy coverage or plan a finished basement.

Older Westborough homes and what water does to them

The state Historical Commission's survey describes Italianate, Second Empire and Queen Anne houses along High Street and worker housing along Milk Street, built in the late industrial period. Homes from that era often have plaster ceilings, old wood framing and basements that were dug long before sump pumps were common.

Wet plaster is heavy, so a ceiling stain that keeps growing deserves an inspection before it comes down. Old hardwood cups when it stays damp. After the water is out, water mitigation with air movers and dehumidifiers is what keeps a house like that from swelling and staining behind the walls.

Utilities and permits in Westborough

Public water comes from the Westborough Water Department, part of the town's Water and Sewer Division at 131 Oak Street. Its latest annual water quality report lists ten wells inside town plus Sandra Pond, also called the Westborough Reservoir. The same division runs the municipal sewer and publishes a sanitary sewer overflow notification plan.

Building permits go through the Building Department in the Forbes Municipal Building at 45 West Main Street. The Conservation Commission administers the state Wetlands Protection Act and the town's own wetlands bylaw, which names flood-prone areas in its purpose. Before a rebuild near a brook or pond, ask both offices whether review applies. Permit rules vary by lot, so ask instead of assuming.

On insurance, the state Division of Insurance says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage and that sewer backup usually needs an added endorsement. Check your own policy or ask your insurer, and report a claim promptly.
