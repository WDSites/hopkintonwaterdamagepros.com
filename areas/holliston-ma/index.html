Through the year: what water does to Holliston homes

Spring is when snowmelt and rain raise brooks and ground water, and a sump pump that works in April can fail in the first hard storm. Summer thunderstorms bring fast runoff to lots that slope toward the house. Fall leaf buildup clogs gutters, and winter freezes put pipes at risk in unheated corners.

Holliston is mostly single-family homes, about 84 percent by the town's housing plan, and the Census puts owner-occupancy at roughly 87 percent. About a third of owner-occupied homes were built in 1959 or earlier. Older foundations and a high water table are a combination worth planning for, which is why basement flooding cleanup is one of the most common calls from this part of town.

If water is still spreading in Holliston

  • Stop what you can stop

    If a supply line, water heater or sump discharge line is the cause, close its valve or unplug the pump. Rising ground water has no valve, so concentrate on protecting belongings.

  • Keep power off wet areas

    Turn off power to wet circuits only if you can do it from dry footing. If you cannot reach the panel safely, call the utility or 911 first, then us.

  • Document before you move

    Take photos of the water line and the damage before lifting anything. Then raise furniture and boxes off the floor.

  • Do not pump a septic overflow yourself

    If septic water is in the house, stay out and keep pets and children away. Do not flush chlorinated water into a septic tank during cleanup.

  • Call the dispatch line

    Call (508) 446-9244 any hour, and tell us what is wet and where the water came from.

Ponds, brooks and culverts with a record

Holliston's 2024 hazard mitigation plan describes several problem spots. The Houghton Pond dam on Jar Brook is town-owned and rated high hazard, and a town engineering report says the Route 16 culvert downstream floods the road and parking lot in storms as small as a five-year event. A heavy storm in 2005 backed water up behind the dam and flooded a residential area.

The plan also says Factory Pond dam backups have flooded the Miller and Placentino school complex, and that Woodland Street overtops in a five-year storm. Lake Winthrop covers 137 acres, according to the state. If your yard drains toward any of these, check the FEMA flood map and ask the town about drainage before you finish a basement.

Holliston participates in the National Flood Insurance Program. The hazard plan counts 54 policies and two repetitive-loss properties in the Hopping Brook watershed, which tells you that flood insurance here is not hypothetical. A standard homeowners policy does not cover flood damage, so check your policy or ask your insurer.

Wells, iron and septic: practical details

The town's five wells, on Washington, Central and Brook Streets, Mohawk Path and Maple Street, draw naturally occurring iron and manganese that the water department treats. The town says that water can stain laundry and fixtures brown. Rust-colored staining after a leak is therefore not automatically a sign of contamination, but it is worth a look.

A November 2024 engineering report proposes sewering 71 downtown properties, and until that happens most homes depend on septic. After a flood, a saturated leach field cannot absorb more water, so a cleanup crew and a septic professional may both be needed. If you use a private well, the state advises treating it as contaminated if the well cap has been underwater and testing before drinking.

Historic districts and older construction

The National Register lists the Thomas Hollis Historic District along Washington Street and the East Holliston Historic District, where Georgian and Federal houses are among the older buildings. Homes of that age often have stone foundations that seep, wood framing that tolerates drying well, and plaster that does not.

If you own one, ask a restoration company to describe the drying plan in plain words before equipment goes in. Our water mitigation page explains what the equipment does and why moisture readings drive the schedule.

Permits and offices in Holliston

Building permits are handled at the Building Department, 703 Washington Street, and the town requires a sign-off from the Health Agent for many projects. The Conservation Commission reviews work within 100 feet of a wetland or 200 feet of a perennial stream under the wetlands bylaw. Rules vary by lot, so ask before a rebuild.
