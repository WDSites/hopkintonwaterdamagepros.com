Milford housing by era, and how each one leaks

The Massachusetts Historical Commission's survey notes Italianate and Second Empire houses concentrated around the town center, and brick multifamily buildings in Prospect Heights built between 1903 and 1913 for Draper Company workers. Milford also built its public buildings from local pink granite, which says something about how long buildings here last.

Long-lived buildings carry old plumbing. In a multifamily building, a leak on the third floor reaches the units below within hours, so shutting the water off at the building level matters as much as extraction. In a single-family house of the same age, the usual surprise is a basement that has quietly taken water for years.

Brooks and the Charles River

Milford's state-funded climate resilience report, completed in 2018, lists flood hazard areas at Godfrey Brook, Hospital Brook, O'Brien Brook, Milford Pond and its dam, Beach and Central Streets, Depot Street at National Street, and the Charles River at Howard Street. It also flags the Church Street to Water Street neighborhood and the area near Milford Regional Medical Center.

The report cites flooding in 2009, 2012, 2013, 2015 and 2018, and a FEMA mitigation grant went toward the Godfrey Brook culvert at Church Street. If your basement sits near one of those watercourses, treat standing water as contaminated and read about flood damage restoration before you start pumping.

If water is still spreading in Milford

  • Shut the water at the source

    Find the shutoff for the fixture, the unit or the whole building. In shared buildings, tell neighbors below you so they can move belongings.

  • Treat the electrical panel with respect

    If the panel or outlets are wet, stay away. Switch off power to wet areas only from a dry spot, and call the utility or 911 for any sparking or gas smell before calling us.

  • Photograph, then move

    Take photos of the water line, ceilings and floors, then lift valuables and wring nothing out. The pictures matter for the insurance claim.

  • Stay out of brook and sewer water

    Water that came up from a drain or a flooded yard is not safe to wade through. Keep children and pets away from it.

  • Call the dispatch line

    Dial (508) 446-9244. Tell us the address, the source and how many floors are wet, and we begin lining up a crew right away.

Something unexpected about Milford's water supply

Milford's water is supplied by the Milford Water Company, and one of its main sources is Echo Lake, which sits in Hopkinton, not Milford. The company's state source water assessment also lists a Charles River intake and wellfields on Dilla Street and Clark Island. It supplies parts of Hopedale and Mendon as well.

That matters to a homeowner for one practical reason. If the town announces a main break or a shutdown, a pressure drop can follow, and a faucet left open during the outage can flood a room when pressure returns. Close fixtures during a shutdown and check the house when service is restored.

Sewer and permit offices

Sewer service is run by the Milford Sewer Department and Board of Sewer Commissioners. The treatment plant is physically in Hopedale and discharges to the Charles River, and the town's own report notes that it sits in a flood-prone location. If a basement drain backs up during a storm, call the sewer department about the line as well as us about the cleanup.

Building permits go through Building and Inspections at Town Hall, 52 Main Street. The Board of Health is in the same building. Permit rules vary, so ask the department before a rebuild instead of assuming. Our water damage repair page explains what rebuild work usually involves once the structure is dry.
