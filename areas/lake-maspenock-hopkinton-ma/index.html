    What Lake Maspenock is

    MassWildlife's pond information lists North (Maspenock) Pond as a 234-acre great pond in Hopkinton, an impoundment of the Mill River about three quarters of a mile west of Route 495. Its average depth is nine feet. Hopkinton's 2017 Master Plan calls it Lake Maspenock, notes that summer cottages were built around it between the wars, and says it drains to the Blackstone River through the Mill River.

    A May 2023 letter in the Hopkinton Independent, signed by residents of the Lake Maspenock neighborhood, shows the name is in local use. We use it for the homes around the pond and do not claim exact boundaries.

    The dam and what is known about it

    The Hopkinton Independent reported in January 2025 that repairs to the Lake Maspenock dam's left downstream wall began in December 2024. The work includes a new concrete wall, riprap and a rebuilt embankment, funded with $400,000 approved at the 2022 Town Meeting plus a state Office of Dam Safety grant. The 2017 hazard plan also lists a dam rebuild as a carried-forward project.

    Dam repair is a safety and maintenance project. It is not evidence that homes flooded, and we do not say that it did.

    Safety first: wet basement near water

    Do these in order

    • ✓Do not enter a basement with standing water if it reaches outlets, the panel or the furnace.
    • ✓Call the utility or 911 about sparks, a burning smell or gas before anything else.
    • ✓Treat water from the ground, a drain or the lake as contaminated and keep children and pets away.
    • ✓If you have a sump pump, check that it is running, but do not stand in water to do it.
    • ✓Photograph the water line from a safe spot and call us. Call a plumber for any broken line.

    Stormwater, runoff and flood maps

    Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. The town's floodplain bylaw follows FEMA's Middlesex County maps, and we did not confirm which lakeside parcels are mapped. Please do not infer flood risk from the pond alone.

    The 2023 letter from neighborhood residents says they opposed a rezoning at South Street and Hayward Street partly over concerns about pollution and stormwater runoff reaching the lake. That is residents' concern, not a measured finding.

    What changes the cost

    Cost follows the water category, how many square feet are wet, the materials and the number of drying days. Groundwater adds cleanup, a finished basement adds scope, and a bare slab dries faster than carpet. The scope and price are explained before work starts.

    Insurance and prevention

    The state Division of Insurance says standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, that seepage through basement walls is excluded, and that sewer and drain backup needs an added endorsement. Check your policy or ask your insurer.

    Keep gutters clear, direct downspouts away from the foundation and test a sump pump before the wet season. None of this guarantees a dry basement.
