The water system and why pressure matters

Southborough's DPW runs the Hosmer and Boland pumping stations and the Clear Hill, Oak Hill and Tara Road tanks. A town evaluation of the Hopkinton interconnection notes that some neighborhoods, such as Fairview and Skylar Drive, run low pressure, while Boston Road near the Sudbury Reservoir can exceed 100 psi and may need a pressure reducing valve.

That is useful to a homeowner. High pressure stresses washing machine hoses, ice maker lines and old fixtures. If you live in a high-pressure area, a pressure reducing valve and braided hoses are cheaper than a water damage cleanup after a burst line.

If water is still spreading in Southborough

  • Shut the main first

    Look for the main shutoff where the water line enters the house, and close it. In a high-pressure area, an open line can empty a lot of water in a short time.

  • Switch off power from a dry spot

    If the water reached outlets or the panel, do not stand in it. Turn off power only if you can reach the panel dry, and call the utility or 911 for any sparking or gas smell.

  • Photograph and lift

    Record the water line and the damage, then lift valuables and rugs. Keep a list of wet items for the insurance claim.

  • Stay clear of dirty water

    Water that came through a failed septic line or a flooded yard may be contaminated. Keep children and pets away.

  • Call the dispatch line

    Call (508) 446-9244, and tell us which fixture failed and which floors are wet.

Reservoir, ponds and brooks

The Sudbury Reservoir covers about 1,240 acres in Southborough and Marlborough, according to the state conservation agency, and the town's annual report says it floods a tenth of the town's area. The reservoir is protected water supply land, so shoreline development is limited.

Smaller waters matter too. The town's state-funded resilience report lists ponds and dams including Lynbrook, Crystal, Upper Crystal, Cordaville and the St. Marks School pond, and says the 2010 storms pushed small streams above flood stage. In 2024, a sinkhole on Willow Street let floodwater top the road twice and close it.

For a homeowner, that history shows how quickly small streams can rise. Check the FEMA flood map through the town's FEMA page, which lists eleven map panels, before you plan a basement renovation. If water does come in, our flood damage restoration service handles contaminated water as a separate category.

Septic systems and what a backup means

Southborough has no municipal sewer, and the town's resilience report lists a plan to study sewer options on Route 9 and alternatives to septic systems. In practice, most properties rely on septic. A saturated leach field after heavy rain can push wastewater back into a house.

If that happens, stop using water, keep clear of the area and call a septic professional and the Board of Health. Cleanup is treated as contaminated water, with protective gear, removal of porous materials and sanitizing.

Villages and landmarks we know

The town's official villages are Southborough Center, Cordaville, Fayville and Southville. The Southborough Center Historic District, home to St. Mark's School and Fay School, is listed on the National Register. Older institutional and village buildings have their own drying challenges, such as thick masonry and original framing.

Fayville has its own ZIP, 01745, which lists Southborough as an accepted city name. We cover both ZIP codes below. Cordaville Road, though, also runs through Ashland, so check your address if you are near the line.
