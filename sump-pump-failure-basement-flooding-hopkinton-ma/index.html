Sump Pump Failure and Basement Flooding in Hopkinton, MA

Why pumps stop, what to do in the first hour and what the state says about coverage.

By the Hopkinton Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026 · Hopkinton, MA

Sump pump failure in Hopkinton, MA turns a dry basement into a flooded one, often during the same storm that raises the water table. A sump pump is a small pump set in a pit in the basement floor that collects groundwater and sends it outside, away from the foundation.

When it stops, whether from a power outage, a stuck switch or a clogged line, groundwater has nowhere to go but up. This guide covers why pumps fail, what to do and how insurance treats it.

Why sump pumps fail when you need them most

Pumps work hardest during heavy rain and spring melt, which is also when outages happen. Most failures fall into a short list of causes. Knowing them tells you what to check before storm season.

Common sump pump failure causes
CauseWhat it looks likeWhat helps
Power outagePump silent during a stormBattery backup or a generator, run safely outdoors
Stuck float switchWater rises but the pump never startsTest by lifting the float or pouring in water
Clogged intake or discharge linePump runs but water does not leaveClear debris and check outside outlet

Worn motorLoud noise, constant running or no responseReplace an aging pump before storm season
Undersized pumpRuns nonstop and still loses groundHave the capacity assessed for your pit

Test the pump a few times a year. Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch it start, run and shut off. Check that water leaves the discharge pipe outside and drains away from the house, not back toward the foundation.

What to do when the pump fails and water rises

  1. Stay out of the water if the basement has outlets, a panel or appliances nearby, and shut off power from a dry location
  2. Check whether the pump has power, the float moves freely and the discharge line is open
  3. Move belongings, boxes and furniture up off the floor
  4. Photograph the water height and the pump before anything is touched
  5. Call for water removal if the water is more than a puddle

Basement groundwater is not clean. It can carry soil, bacteria and anything stored on the floor. The Massachusetts home care guide says to assume pathogens are present, to wear gloves, eye protection and boots, and to discard food, medicines, mattresses, carpet padding and cardboard that got wet.

Why removing the water quickly matters

Basement walls and slabs can take on moisture quickly. Drywall wicks water upward from the bottom, wood framing absorbs it and stored items start to fail. The state health guide says wet areas should be completely dried in 24 hours and that leaks and floods should be dried within 48 hours under the Housing Code.

When the ground outside is saturated, pumping out a deep basement all at once can stress walls and the floor slab. Professionals check conditions outside and remove water in stages. Our flood damage restoration page describes how flooded basements are handled from removal to rebuild.

Local note

Groundwater, wetlands and Hopkinton basements

The town's 2004 housing plan, hosted on mass.gov, says wetlands and wastewater disposal are significant land use constraints in many parts of Hopkinton.

The Cedar Swamp ACEC, about 1,650 acres shared with Westborough, is mostly wetland that stores floodwater for the Sudbury River basin. For a homeowner near a wetland edge, brook or pond, the practical point is that a high water table can leave a sump pump running often, so a backup pump or battery is worth considering before the next storm.

What the state says about insurance

The Division of Insurance states that sump pump failure is excluded from standard policies unless you added an endorsement. Seepage through basement walls is also not covered, and no endorsement exists for it. Sewer and drain backup is excluded too, though most insurers offer an endorsement.

Flood from outside is a separate matter. Standard policies do not cover flood, and flood policies usually take effect 30 days after purchase. The National Flood Insurance Program limits coverage in basements. Check your policy or ask your insurer which category your event falls into, and report the loss immediately.

Document first

Photograph the water height, the pump, the discharge line and the damaged belongings before cleanup. The state advises photographing damage and water height before cleanup begins.

Drying a basement after the water is out

Pumping is the first step, not the last. Concrete holds moisture, and finished walls hide wet insulation and wood. Moisture meters show how far water reached, and dehumidifiers pull humidity from the air. Our water mitigation page explains how drying is measured until the readings are normal.

Dry quickly and watch for mold. The state recommends finding and fixing the moisture source, discarding contaminated wallboard and insulation after a flood and not painting or caulking over mold. Environmental mold testing is not recommended as part of water damage cleanup.

Preventing the next failure

Pre-storm sump pump checklist

  • ✓Test the pump with a bucket of water
  • ✓Confirm the discharge pipe drains well away from the foundation
  • ✓Clear the pit of silt and debris
  • ✓Consider a battery backup for power outages
  • ✓Keep stored items off the floor on shelves or pallets
  • ✓Ask your agent about a sump pump and sewer backup endorsement
