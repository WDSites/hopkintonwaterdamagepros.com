How to Get Rid of Mold in Hopkinton, MA

What you can do yourself, what to leave alone, and the line where a professional should take over.

By the Hopkinton Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026 · Hopkinton, MA

Knowing how to get rid of mold in Hopkinton, MA begins with two ideas. First, small patches on hard surfaces can often be cleaned by a homeowner. Second, no cleaning lasts unless the moisture that fed the mold is fixed. Mold removal means taking growth off or out of a building and correcting the damp conditions behind it. State guidance shows where the do-it-yourself line sits, which small jobs suit a careful homeowner, and when to call a trained professional.

Step zero is always the water

DPH puts it simply. To stop mold growth, you need to find out where the water or moisture is coming from and fix it. If a pipe drips, a roof leaks or a basement stays damp, scrubbing only gives mold a short break.

So before you open a bottle of cleaner, check for an active leak, confirm that the area is drying, and think about ventilation. Bathrooms need fans that actually vent, and basements may need a dehumidifier. If you cannot find the source, that is a reason to call someone, not a reason to clean harder.

The 10 square foot line

The state guide notes that EPA recommends having professionals handle mold areas larger than 10 square feet. That is roughly a patch three feet by three feet. Below that, a careful homeowner can often manage. Above it, or when growth is inside a wall, in an air duct or on materials that soak up water, plan on help.

Also be cautious if the mold followed sewage or contaminated flood water. Those jobs bring other hazards and need a different approach.

How to clean a small patch on a hard surface

DPH says mold can generally be cleaned with detergent and water, and it usually does not recommend bleach. That surprises people, but it fits the facts. Cleaning means physically removing the growth, not just changing its color.

  1. Fix the moisture source and let the area dry.
  2. Put on gloves, eye protection and a mask or respirator suited to the job. Keep other people, children and pets out of the room.
  3. Scrub the surface with detergent and water using a rag or brush. Do not dry-brush or sweep, which sends spores into the air.
  4. Rinse or wipe with clean water and dry the surface completely, using a fan or dehumidifier.
  5. Bag the rags and any waste before taking them through the house.
  6. Recheck in a few days. If growth comes back, there is still moisture.

Never mix bleach and ammonia

DPH specifically warns not to mix bleach and ammonia. That includes household cleaners that contain either one, so read labels. Pick one product, use it as directed, never combine cleaners and keep the room ventilated.

What not to do

  • Do not paint or caulk over mold. DPH is direct on this point.
  • Do not try to keep wet drywall, insulation or wood trim that has been contaminated. DPH advises discarding contaminated porous materials such as these after a flood.
  • Do not use a leaf blower, vacuum or broom on dry mold. Disturbing it spreads spores.
  • Do not sand or scrape painted surfaces without thinking about lead. The state guide notes that disturbing more than 6 square feet of painted interior surface brings lead renovation rules into play.
  • Do not assume a stain is harmless because it is small. Check behind it.

Protecting people in the house

DPH lists health concerns separately for a reason. People with asthma, chronic respiratory problems, allergies or weakened immune systems may react seriously to mold, and they, along with children and pets, should stay away from cleanup. Symptoms the state lists include a runny nose, sneezing, coughing, wheezing and burning eyes.

If someone in your home is in one of these groups, think twice about doing even a small cleanup yourself.

When it is time to hand it off

Call for help if the patch is large, if you cannot find where the water is coming from, if the mold is inside a wall or beneath flooring, or if someone in the house is sensitive. A professional mold remediation job uses containment, filtered air and careful removal so spores are not pushed into the rest of the house. If the room is still wet, water damage cleanup comes first.
