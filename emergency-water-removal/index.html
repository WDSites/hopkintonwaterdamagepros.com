When standing water is an emergency

Water on a floor soaks into carpet, padding, drywall and wood within minutes, and the longer it sits the further it travels. Water is an emergency when it is spreading, when it is above floor level, when it is near electrical equipment, or when it is coming from a source you cannot stop.

If you can see sparks, smell gas or hear buzzing from the panel, leave and call the utility or 911 before calling a restoration company. Safety comes before saving belongings.

What to do in the first ten minutes

A short list while you wait for a crew

  • ✓Close the main shutoff, usually where the water line enters the basement or utility room.
  • ✓If you cannot find it, close the shutoff for the fixture or appliance that is leaking.
  • ✓Turn off power to the wet area only if you can reach the breaker without standing in water.
  • ✓Photograph the water and the damage for the insurance claim before moving things.
  • ✓Lift furniture legs onto blocks and move rugs, papers and electronics out of the water.
  • ✓Keep people and pets away from the water, especially if it could be contaminated.

How extraction works

Extraction uses submersible pumps for deep water and extraction units, similar to powerful wet vacuums, for carpet and shallow water. Technicians remove water in passes, lift carpet to pull water from the padding and check the subfloor for what soaked through.

Extraction is the first stage, not the last. After the bulk water is out, the structure is still wet, so drying follows through water mitigation. If the water was contaminated, cleanup and sanitizing follow through flood damage restoration.

Burst pipe water damage

A burst pipe is the classic emergency, and in Hopkinton it often shows up after a cold snap. Water in a frozen pipe expands, the pipe splits, and the leak starts when the ice melts. The damage can be hidden inside a wall for hours before anyone sees a stain.

Closing the main valve is the most important step. Then open a faucet at the lowest point to drain remaining water and call a plumber for the repair. A restoration crew handles the water that is already out, and a plumber fixes the pipe.

Other common sources include washing machine hoses, water heaters, dishwasher lines and ice maker lines. A plastic supply line under a sink can split without warning. If you find water under a sink, close the valve under it, then check the cabinet floor and wall behind it, because water often runs into hidden spaces.

Why Hopkinton homes see this call

Hopkinton's water comes from five town wells, including wells off Pond Street near Whitehall Reservoir and wells west of Fruit Street, according to the town's source water assessment. The town says its iron content can leave an orange or brown tint and stains, so discolored water is not always a leak, but a damp ceiling or floor always deserves a look.

Many homes in Hopkinton have finished basements, and a failed sump pump or a heavy storm can fill one quickly. The Division of Insurance notes that standard policies often exclude sump pump failure unless an endorsement is added, so check your policy before you need it.
