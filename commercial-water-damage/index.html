This page explains what that means in practice, what a building owner or tenant can do before help arrives and which local offices matter when walls, ceilings and floors are rebuilt after the water is gone.

What commercial dispatch covers

Dispatch means we take your call, learn what is wet and arrange a crew for the right stage of the job. The stages are the same as at home: remove the water, dry the structure, clean what was affected and coordinate the rebuild.

We do not describe specialties we cannot back up. If a job needs a licensed trade, such as a plumber, an electrician or a roofer, we say so and coordinate around them.

Who owns what: landlord and tenant

In a leased space, the first question is who is responsible for which part. The landlord usually owns the building shell, the roof, the base building plumbing and the common areas. A tenant often owns the contents, the fit-out, and sometimes the interior improvements.

Read your lease before you call, and notify the landlord or property manager as soon as water appears. The party who owns the damaged items usually decides how they are dried or replaced, so confirm who is authorizing the work.

First hour and safety in a commercial space

Safety in the first hour

  • ✓Know where the main water shut-off is, and make sure staff and the property manager know it as well.
  • ✓Call the utility or 911 for sparks, buzzing, smoke or a gas smell before anything else.
  • ✓Do not walk through standing water near panels, outlets, cords or equipment racks.
  • ✓Keep people out of the affected area, and mark it clearly.
  • ✓Call a plumber, roofer or HVAC contractor to stop the source, because restoration crews do not repair the source.

Public water problems are different. The Town DPW lists after-hours water emergencies through Fire and Police Dispatch, according to the town's DPW page.

Keeping operations moving, in general terms

Water loss interrupts a business, and a few steps help. Make a quick inventory of records, stock and equipment that are wet or at risk. Move paper files, servers and valuable stock to a dry area when it is safe to do so.

Record the damage and the water line in writing before things are moved, and note what was lifted or discarded. Decide who will talk to the landlord, the insurer and the crew, so one person keeps the record.

Commercial materials behave differently

Common commercial materials and how they usually fare
MaterialWhat water doesUsual approach
Suspended ceiling tilesAbsorb water, sag and stain, and can drop onto people and equipmentWet tiles are removed and the grid and insulation above are checked
Carpet tileWater travels under tiles and into the glue and slabTiles lift individually, and the floor underneath is dried

Because these materials dry at different speeds, drying is measured with moisture meters and often a thermal imaging camera. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers do the drying, and extraction units pull up standing water. The industry reference for this work is ANSI/IICRC S500.

Restoration scope versus rebuild

Restoration covers removing water, drying and cleaning. Rebuild covers the new drywall, ceiling grid, flooring, paint and finish carpentry that follow once the space is dry. Rebuild coordination means arranging the sequence of those trades so the space can reopen.

Separating the two on paper helps. It makes clear what the insurer is looking at, what the landlord is responsible for and what has to be done before the next trade can start. See water damage repair for how rebuilding follows drying.

Permits and local access

Rebuilding often needs permits. According to the town's Inspectional Services page, that office at Town Hall, 3rd floor, 18 Main Street, handles building, electrical, plumbing and gas permits. The same page says building permits become invalid if work does not begin within 180 days.

If the space is in a neighboring town, the local building office decides. For example, Milford lists Building and Inspections at Town Hall, 52 Main Street, and Holliston lists its Building Department at 703 Washington Street. Ask before opening walls or replacing wiring.

For deliveries and crew access, check current conditions on Route 135 and around I-495. MassDOT is advancing the I-495 and I-90 interchange improvements project in Hopkinton, according to its public meeting notice, so plan loading and parking around it.

Commercial insurance is not a homeowners policy

Commercial policies differ from homeowners policies. Coverage, deductibles and business interruption terms vary, and the state's published homeowners guidance does not describe them. Read your policy and ask your insurer or broker what applies.

Report the loss promptly, document the damage and keep invoices. We make no statement about what a policy will pay.
