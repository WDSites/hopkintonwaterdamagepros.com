Cleanup is the work of removing what water ruined and making the rest of the space clean. It sits between pumping the water out and rebuilding, and it is where most of the decisions about saving or throwing away happen.

The first 48 hours: an emergency window

The Massachusetts Department of Public Health advises drying wet areas within 24 hours, and the state's Housing Code requires leak and flood areas to be dried within 48 hours. After that, damp porous materials are likely to start growing mold and giving off odors.

That is why cleanup does not wait for a convenient time. Carpet padding, drywall, insulation and upholstered furniture are the usual casualties, and the sooner they come out, the less spreads to materials that could have been saved.

What cleanup includes

Debris and contents removal

Soaked carpet, padding, drywall and ruined belongings are removed and hauled away.

Cleaning surfaces

Floors, framing and hard surfaces are washed with detergent so that dirt and residue do not stay behind.

Sanitizing

Surfaces that touched contaminated water are disinfected after cleaning.

Deodorizing

Odors are addressed after the source is removed and the area is dry, because masking a smell does not fix it.

Safety before you start cleaning

  • ✓Turn off power to the wet area only if you can reach the panel while standing dry.
  • ✓Wear gloves, boots and eye protection around dirty water, and wash hands afterward.
  • ✓Keep children, pets and anyone with breathing problems out of the area.
  • ✓Do not mix bleach and ammonia or other cleaners.

What can be saved and what cannot

Hard, non-porous materials such as glass, metal and sealed tile are usually cleaned and kept. Porous materials soak up water and anything in it. The Massachusetts environmental agency's home care guide says to always discard food, medicines, mattresses, pillows, upholstered furniture and carpet padding that touched contaminated water, and usually large carpets, books and paper.

Clean-water leaks are more forgiving. Carpet that was lifted quickly and dried can sometimes be kept, and solid wood furniture often dries without harm. Contents that cannot be cleaned on site can sometimes go out for specialty cleaning.

Local context in Hopkinton

Hopkinton Center has a local historic district around the Town Common and the south side of Main Street, established in 1979, and the Woodville Historic District is overseen by its own commission. Homes and buildings in a historic district may have rules about exterior changes, so a cleanup that touches exterior walls should start with a call to the town's Historic District Commission.

Inside, older houses often have plaster, wood trim and original flooring that need a careful drying plan. That affects how much is removed, so ask for a written scope that lists what will come out and why.

Safety, odors and the insurance record

Wear gloves and boots if you must enter a wet area, avoid mixing cleaning chemicals, and never run a gas-powered pump or generator in the house. The state's mold guide says bleach is generally not recommended for mold and that bleach and ammonia must never be mixed.

Photograph everything before it is thrown out, and keep a list of what was removed. Standard homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental damage but not flood, so check your policy and report a claim promptly.
