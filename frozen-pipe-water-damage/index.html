Frozen pipe water damage in Hopkinton, MA usually shows up when the ice melts, not when the pipe freezes. Water becomes the problem the moment a split opens. A frozen pipe is a line where ice has blocked the flow and may be straining the pipe wall. Why the flood comes at thaw

Water expands when it freezes. The ice plug pushes against the pipe wall and the weak spot, often a fitting or a thin section, splits. While the pipe stays frozen, the ice acts as a stopper and nothing leaks.

When the house warms, the ice melts and the system, still under pressure, pours water out of the split. That is why people often discover the damage after a cold night ends, or after power and heat come back during a storm. A burst that already happened is covered in our burst pipe water damage cleanup guide.

Safety first: heat, water and electricity

If a pipe is frozen or just thawed

  • ✓Never use a torch, a propane heater or any open flame on a pipe. It can ignite framing or insulation, and the heat can crack the pipe further.
  • ✓Keep the faucet on that line open, so melting water has somewhere to go and pressure does not build.
  • ✓Know where the main shutoff is, and close it at the first sign of water from a split.
  • ✓Do not touch electrical devices, cords or outlets near wet floors. Leave and call 911 or the utility for sparks or a gas smell.
  • ✓Call a plumber for the pipe and a restoration crew for the water. They do different jobs.

A frozen pipe that has not burst yet

A trickle or no water from one tap, and frost on an exposed pipe, usually means a frozen section. Keep the heat on, open the cabinet doors under sinks, and let the faucet stay open a little.

Gentle warmth, such as warm towels wrapped around an exposed pipe, can help in some cases. Never leave any heat source unattended near wet areas. If you cannot find the frozen section, or the pipe is inside a wall or ceiling, stop and call a plumber.

What you see and what it means

  • A stain that grows on a ceiling after the weather warms: a split above is leaking as the ice melts.
  • The sound of running water with no taps open: close the main and watch the meter or listen again.
  • Bulging, bowed or frost-coated pipe: ice is inside and the pipe is under strain.
  • A damp exterior wall in a cold room: water may be moving along the pipe path inside the cavity.
  • A musty smell days after a cold snap: wet insulation is holding moisture.

Winter in Hopkinton and what the records say

The National Weather Service's 1991 to 2020 normals for Worcester, a regional station and not Hopkinton itself, give an annual snowfall of about 72.9 inches. That is a lot of cold and many freeze and thaw cycles in the region each season.

Federal records show winter storms affecting this part of the state. FEMA-4214-DR covers a severe winter storm, snowstorm and flooding on January 26 to 28, 2015, and lists Middlesex County among the counties eligible for emergency work and snow assistance. FEMA-4379-DR covers the winter storm of March 13 and 14, 2018, and the Federal Register designated Middlesex County for Public Assistance.

Those are county level declarations, not a count of frozen pipes in town. They simply show that heavy winter weather is part of the regional record.

Preventing the freeze

Insulate what is exposed

Pipes in garages, crawl spaces, exterior walls and unheated basements are the usual freeze points. Foam sleeves are a common cover.

Heat tape, used as directed

Electric heat cable made for pipes can protect a vulnerable run. Follow the product instructions and have a plumber or electrician advise.

Let a faucet drip

On a very cold night, a slow drip on a vulnerable line keeps water moving.

Know the shutoff

Everyone in the house should know where the main valve is and how to close it.

Keep heat on while away

A cold, empty house is where a thaw leak runs for hours before anyone notices.

How frozen pipe damage is dried

After the plumber stops the leak, extraction units remove standing water and lift saturated carpet. Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera find water in walls and ceilings, since the split is often inside a cavity.

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure, and readings guide when equipment can come out. The industry reference is ANSI/IICRC S500. If insulation was soaked, it is usually removed because it holds water against framing. For the drying details, see water mitigation.

Insurance and frozen pipes

The state Division of Insurance says water damage not caused by flooding, such as a burst pipe, falls under a standard homeowners policy that usually covers sudden and accidental damage. Neglect and wear and tear are generally not covered.

Ask your insurer how a freeze loss is handled and whether your policy sets any conditions. Check your policy before you assume. Note what you did to stop the water and when.
