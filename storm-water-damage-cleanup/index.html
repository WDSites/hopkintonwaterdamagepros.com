How storm water gets into a house

Storms find the weak points. Wind-driven rain gets behind siding, under window sills and past door thresholds that stay dry in an ordinary shower. Shingles lift, flashing bends, and a fallen limb can open a hole in a roof or a wall in seconds.

Gutters add to it. When they back up with leaves or ice, water pours over the edge and soaks the ground next to the foundation. A nor'easter style storm that lasts two or three days gives every one of those weak points time to leak.

Power outages and sump pump failure

A sump pump is only as dependable as the power feeding it. When the grid goes down in the middle of a storm, the pump stops while the ground is at its wettest. A basement can start taking water in a couple of hours.

A battery backup or a water-powered backup pump is the usual defense, and a generator must stay outdoors and well away from windows. After the power returns, check that the pump runs and that the discharge line is not blocked or frozen.

Safety after a storm comes first

Before you touch anything wet

  • ✓Treat any downed line as live, stay away and call the utility or 911.
  • ✓Do not touch outlets, cords or the panel if you are standing in water or the floor is wet.
  • ✓Never run a generator, grill or pressure washer inside the house or garage.
  • ✓Stay out from under a ceiling that sags or has water pooled above it.
  • ✓Wear boots and gloves around debris, and keep children and pets out of wet rooms.

Storm water or groundwater flooding

The two often arrive together, but the cleanup differs. Storm water through the roof, walls or windows is usually rainwater and can be dried like any other leak. Water that rises through the slab or over the foundation carries soil and may be contaminated, and it is handled as flood work.

If the water came up from the ground, see flood damage restoration. If it came down through the roof, roof leak water damage explains what the ceiling and attic need.

What the record shows for Hopkinton's area

Hopkinton's zoning bylaw includes a Floodplain District for the National Flood Insurance Program, and the town's Conservation Administrator is the designated Floodplain Administrator. That is the office to ask before you build or repair in a mapped flood hazard area.

Federal disaster declarations for flooding in May 2006 and in March 2010 each listed Middlesex County for individual assistance, according to the Federal Register and the White House archive. Those lists are by county and do not single out Hopkinton, but they show that wide regional flooding has happened.

FEMA's page for FEMA-4214-DR covers the severe winter storm, snowstorm and flooding of January 26 to 28, 2015, with Middlesex among the counties eligible for emergency work. A multi-day storm can put wind, snow load and melt on the same roof.

What cleanup involves

A crew first finds where the water went, using a moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera to read walls and ceilings. Standing water is pulled out with extraction units and pumps. Wet carpet padding, soaked insulation and swollen drywall come out, and debris from the storm is cleared from the work area.

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers then dry the structure. The industry reference for this process is ANSI/IICRC S500. Drying is finished when readings match a dry reference, which is why equipment stays in place after the room feels dry.

Document it before it is moved

Before anything is thrown out, record what you can: the water line on the walls, the damaged items and the source of the entry. Write down the date and time the storm started and when the power went out. Keep every receipt for tarps, pumps and emergency repairs.

Insurance and storm damage

The Division of Insurance says standard homeowners policies usually cover sudden and accidental damage but not flood. Sump pump failure and sewer backup are generally excluded unless you added an endorsement. Flood coverage is a separate policy, and most take about 30 days to start. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and report the loss as soon as you can.

Mistakes people make after a storm

  • Waiting for the rain to stop before pulling up wet carpet or padding.
  • Using a household wet vacuum on a flooded basement with live outlets nearby.
  • Patching the roof or window and leaving the wet wall behind it alone.
  • Throwing out damaged items before recording them for the insurance claim.
