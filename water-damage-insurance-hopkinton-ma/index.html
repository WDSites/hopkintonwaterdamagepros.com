Water Damage Insurance in Hopkinton, MA

What a standard policy usually covers, what it leaves out, and how to document a loss.

By the Hopkinton Water Damage Pros team · Published 2026 · Hopkinton, MA

Water damage insurance in Hopkinton, MA works differently depending on where the water came from. In Massachusetts, a standard homeowners policy usually covers water damage that is sudden and accidental, such as a burst pipe, but it does not cover flood.

Water damage insurance, in plain terms, is the part of your policy that pays for losses from covered water events, and the cause decides whether it applies. This post explains the main categories using guidance from the state Division of Insurance, so you know what to check before you call anyone.

Sudden and accidental versus everything else

The Division of Insurance says water damage that is not caused by flooding, such as a burst pipe, falls under the standard policy, which usually covers sudden and accidental damage. The phrase matters. A pipe that splits overnight is sudden. A slow drip behind a wall that has run for months is not.

The state also says damage from wear and tear or neglect is not covered. That is why a claim often turns on what happened and how long it went on. Photos of the failed part, the date you found the problem and proof you acted quickly all help show the loss was sudden.

Flood is a separate product

Standard homeowners, renters and condo policies do not cover water damage from flood, the state says. Flood coverage needs a separate flood policy or a limited endorsement. Massachusetts does not require flood insurance, though lenders usually do when a property is in a mapped flood zone.

Most flood policies take effect 30 days after purchase. There is usually a waiting period, so buying a policy once rain is in the forecast does not help. The exception is a policy bought along with a loan closing. The National Flood Insurance Program also does not pay for loss of use or additional living expenses, and its basement coverage is limited.

Sewer backup and sump pump endorsements

Standard residential policies do not cover sewer backup, but most insurers offer endorsements, and the state says the coverage can be bought for a nominal premium. Read the wording carefully. Some endorsements still exclude backups that happen because of flooding.

Sump pump failure follows the same pattern. It is excluded unless you add an endorsement. If you have a basement and a sump pump, ask your agent whether the pump and the water it moves are covered, and whether the endorsement covers a power outage that stops the pump.

Local note

Hopkinton and the flood map

Hopkinton appears on FEMA's Massachusetts community status list under the name Hopkinton, Town of, and the town's zoning bylaw sets up a Floodplain District for special flood hazard areas, tied to administration of the National Flood Insurance Program. For you, the practical step is simple. Ask the town or your agent whether your address sits in a mapped flood zone, because that decides whether a lender may require flood coverage and whether a policy is worth pricing.

How to document a loss before cleanup

The state advises photographing the damage and the water height before cleanup. Do that first, even in the middle of the stress. A phone camera is enough. Insurers evaluate what they can see, and removed carpet and drywall cannot be photographed afterward.

What to gather for your claim

  • ✓Wide and close photos of every wet room before anything is removed
  • ✓A note of the date and time you first noticed the water
  • ✓A photo of the source, such as a failed fitting or a backed-up drain
  • ✓A list of damaged belongings with approximate ages
  • ✓Written scope and invoices from any company that dries or cleans
  • ✓The names and dates of every call with your agent or insurer

Report quickly and keep mitigating

The Division of Insurance says to report a claim immediately to your agent or company. Most policies also expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage, so starting emergency water removal and drying is usually part of protecting your claim, not a separate choice. Check your policy or ask your insurer exactly what it expects.

Keep damaged items if the insurer wants to see them, or photograph them thoroughly before disposal. Save every receipt for temporary equipment, hotel stays and supplies. If something about the process seems unclear, the Division of Insurance Consumer Service Unit takes questions at 617-521-7794.

What a restoration company can and cannot do for a claim

A restoration company dries the building and records what it did. That record, with moisture readings, photos and a written scope, is useful when you talk to your insurer. We do not decide coverage, and no contractor can promise that a claim will be approved. That decision belongs to your insurer and your policy.
